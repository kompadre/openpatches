## 2026-11-17: The Player — one Playhead per track

The transport used to map every tick onto a stateless sequence and fire what was there. Now a **Player** (`html/synth/player.js`) owns *where every track is* and *what fires*; the transport keeps the clock, the modes, external sync and MIDI clock out, and hands firing and locating over. Position state lives in the Player, in memory, never in the project or the sync. Five phases, each committed on its own (SESSION.md kept the decisions; this is the sum).

**Heads, loops and the Section's end** (phase 1). Every track with a clip in the scope has a **head**; heads walk together one row per tick, gather the notes under them, and over a gap between clips keep stepping silently. At the **exact end of its track's last clip** — never rounded to the bar — a head wraps to the scope's first row and is **looped**; a short track therefore loops against the longer ones (a 12-column cut against a bar is 3 over 4, which is the point). A track with no clip in the scope has no head. The Section ends on the tick a head sits on its last row *not yet looped* while every other head has looped, is stopped, or sits on its own last row (so equal tracks leave together); then every head is sent to row −1 of the next scope — the next pass or part of the piece, or the Section again — lands on row 0 with the looped status cleared, and that tick carries the recorder's `seam`. The heads run **one row ahead of the clock**: a head at row N has armed N's notes for the next tick. `seek` (`player.start(col)`) replays the walk from the top; the transport seeks whenever a tick's worker count is not the one it expected (first tick, external `continue`, a skipped tick). **Behaviour changes, accepted:** a short track no longer falls silent after its last clip but loops; a section whose longest track ends mid-bar ends there; the piece's total length is nominal.

**Explicit notes** (phase 2). The Player emits typed `on`/`off` events and keeps a sounding registry counting **real ticks**, so a seek or jump never cuts a note; the one synthetic off is the Section transition's all-notes-off, ordered ahead of row 0's ons. One output interface (`html/synth/note-output.js`) replaces the bare `playNote`: ons held open (`sustained`), offs via the stop the chain returns (promise-aware), `allOff()` on Stop. A stale tick passes only the offs. Tails that rang across a loop restart are now cut at the transition.

**Recording through the Playhead** (phase 3). A take writes into **the track on screen** and at the column **its head** was on at that tick — the transport keeps a take log of head rows per tick, so a looped short track records into its own bar. `activeRecordSlot` is gone: the recorder's slot is the roll's active slot (`pianoroll.getActiveSlot()`), for audition, note-off and the take alike — and with it the long-standing "a live take can land on the wrong track" bug. The keyboard's voice select now moves the roll and the tracker directly (the bank skips voiceless slots).

**Per-track moves from the effects column** (phase 4). One registry (`html/synth/tracker-commands.js`) with **two names per command**: the stored letter is FastTracker II's where FT2 has the effect, the human-readable name is what the status line speaks (`commandByName` takes either). `B` **jump** (FT2 Bxx) to row N · `D` **break** (FT2 Dxx): the Section ends after this row · `R` **back** N rows · `K` **skip** N rows · `S` **stop**: the track rests until the next Section. Arguments count rows. A command is read when the head arrives at its row and takes effect on its next step; a backward or past-the-end move is a loop; a stopped head counts as satisfied; `break` decides the vote. Unknown letters stay stored, draw dim, and are ignored. Mid-play edits no longer re-seek by a nominal column: the rebuilt Player **restores** the old one's state (heads, clock, sounding). The position broadcast carries `heads`; the **Tracker** draws a line per track and the **Section pane** a marker per lane where a head differs from the global playhead (orange; grey when stopped). The roll already followed the edited track's head.

**Humanize** (phase 5). `track.humanize = {timing, velocity}`, from the Tracker head's **≈** button (off → ¼ → ½ → 1, Shift back). Velocity moves up to ±32·velocity, timing up to ±½ row·timing, converted at the live tempo when scheduled. Early notes ride the one-row lookahead — handed out a tick ahead and scheduled before their tick — late ones after it, both from the tick's due time; un-nudged notes still fire on the tick; the transition's offs precede an early row-0 note; Stop cancels pending nudges. Seeded as a hybrid: (track, row, **pass since Play**, seed) — every pass breathes differently, every Play repeats the performance; one `seed` knob away from varying every time.

**Golden capture** (phase 0, `test/golden-capture.js`, `test/golden/*.json`, `node test/golden/update.js`): today's sequencers pinned per column over twelve fixtures; the four mixed-length fixtures carry the expected delta under the loop rule, which phase 1 met.

## 2026-11-17: Tracker velocity and effects columns; playback honours velocity

The Player's testing playground (TODO.md › Player), ahead of it. Reverses the 2026-11-07 "note-ons and note-offs only".

**Velocity column, per lane.** Every expanded sub-lane is now a note cell (46px) plus a velocity cell (26px). The velocity lives on the note (`note.vel`, 1–127, omitted at the default 100 — a pattern of plain notes stores exactly what it did), so every placement of the pattern shares it. On the velocity cell, digits roll the value (`8`,`0` → 80, clamped at 127), Delete returns the default; the default draws dim, a set value plain. Copy/cut/paste and block moves carry it; `mergeDuplicates` keeps the kept note's. Over collab the note atom's value grows from bare `dur` to `{dur, vel}` only when a velocity is set, and a malformed one is dropped on apply.

**Effects column, one per track**, at the track's right edge (34px), compact or expanded. A letter A–Z is the command, digits roll the argument (0–999), Delete clears the row. Commands are stored on the **section**, per track (`section.fx[trackId] = [{row, cmd, arg}]`, sorted, one per row) — not in patterns: a command marks the track's timeline (it works in the gap between clips) and a shared pattern must not drag commands to every placement. `createSection` always carries `fx` (collab's refill deletes what the factory omits); it travels as the section's `fx` field atom, whole, last write wins. The command set is open — the Player gives commands their meaning (the first will be "move this track's position").

**Playback honours velocity end to end.** The sequencer's events carry `vel`; the transport passes `{velocity}` through `playNote`; the MIDI out already took it, and the internal synth path (`playNoteOnChannel`, `playNoteRealtime`) now receives it instead of silently playing 100. A MIDI-in take records its velocity onto the note too (`activePresses` keeps it; 100 is not stored); mouse and keyboard presses stay at the default.

The cursor knows its sub-cell (`cursor.sub`) and the FX column (`lane === viewLanes`); arrows walk note → velocity → next lane → FX → next track. Selections, stamps and moves stay on whole lanes (note + velocity together) and never include the FX column.

## 2026-11-15: Piece blocks drag, take keys, and have a menu

**Drag a block** to move it in the piece: past 4 px the block dims and a white marker shows where it lands. The drop position is decided by `dropIndex(rects, x, y)`, a pure export, because the strip wraps: it looks in the pointer's row (or the nearest one) and drops before the first block whose middle is right of the pointer, otherwise after the row's last block. **Alt-drag places the section again** at the drop point (reuse), and the marker turns green. Escape cancels. A click after a drag does not also pick a block. **Keys on a focused block:** Alt+←/→ move it. Delete or Backspace take it out of the piece with no confirm, since the section stays; the next block is then picked, so repeated Deletes walk the piece. The block handles these keys itself, so the grid never deletes a selected clip from them, and focus comes back after the re-render. **Right-click a block** for its menu: ⎘ Make a copy, − Remove from piece, ✕ Remove section… (confirms, and is disabled for the last section). Escape or a click outside closes it.

The ◀ ▶ buttons, − From piece, ⎘ and ✕ now share the same functions as the keys and the menu. Taking a block out with the button or the menu keeps its section on screen; only Delete moves on to the next block. **`moveSection` is retired** from the model and the session; `moveEntry` is the only reorder.

## 2026-11-15: The piece has its own play order — sections can be reused

**`project.arrangement`** is the order the piece plays in, as entries `{ id, sectionId }`; **`project.sections`** becomes the library, where a section may sit unused. A section can be placed any number of times (a chorus three times is three entries, one section; editing it changes all three). Entries have their own ids because each placement is moved, removed or copied on its own, and the piece playhead needs to say *which* chorus is playing.

**Schema 2.** A project without an arrangement — every schema-1 body and file — gets every section once in its current order, so it plays exactly as before. An explicit empty arrangement is kept (nothing placed yet). Entries naming a missing section, or repeating an id, are dropped by `normalizeProject`. A schema-1 reader refuses a schema-2 file instead of silently losing the repeats.

**Model:** `placeSection(id, at)` (reuse), `moveEntry(entryId, to)`, `removeEntry(entryId)` (out of the piece; the section stays), `copyEntry(entryId)` ("this chorus is different": the entry plays a fresh copy, the others keep the original), `sectionUsage(id)`. `addSection` places at the end unless `{ place: false }`; `duplicateSection` places its copy after the original's first entry; `removeSection` takes every entry with it. `moveSection` is transitional: it moves the section in the library and its first entry in the piece, which is exactly the old behaviour while every section is placed once. Sections gain a `color` (stable per id, from a muted `SECTION_COLORS` set apart from the patterns'), for the Piece panel.

**Everything that reads the order follows it:** `pieceLayout` walks the arrangement (a section placed twice is two parts, each carrying its `entry`); the piece sequence's `locate`/`localize` return `entryId`; the transport's "anything to play?" for the piece reads placed sections only; export writes `arrangement`. Collab: part sync carries the new top-level key unchanged. The op layer gets an `arrangement` atom, carried whole with last write wins. A section created or deleted groups with it. A new invariant flags an entry whose section is gone. Session: `placeSection`, `moveEntry`, `removeEntry`, `copyEntry` (the copy becomes the edited section), `sectionUsage`, each a `section` event.

No visible change: the Piece panel is the next phase (TODO, "The Piece panel").

## 2026-11-14: Ducking follows the transient; the Mixer's FX control is one button

**Ducking let go far too slowly, and held flat for a whole note.** Two causes, both measured against the real `msfa.wasm`. `BlockCoef` returned `exp(-block/ms)` — a one-pole *time constant*, so a dialled 180 ms release took ~2.5x that to become inaudible: an 81 ms kick produced 540 ms of ducking. And `DuckGain`'s target was a gate (`db > threshold ? amount : 0`), so the full depth was held for as long as the source stayed over a −30 dBFS threshold, which for a patch that sustains is the note's whole gate length — the duck read as following note duration rather than the hit. Fixed by scaling the coefficient to a ~95 % recovery (`-3.0f * block_period / ms`) and by making the target track how far over the threshold the source actually is. Measured end to end, same dialled 180 ms: audible duck 540 → 241 ms, >3 dB 341 → 176 ms. `BlockCoef` is shared, so the per-channel compressor's attack and release now mean what they say too; its envelope is still block-rate (~1.45 ms), so it remains glue and not a limiter.

**The Mixer strip's four FX letters (D O C R) became one `FX` button.** Four 0.55 rem glyphs on an 86 px strip was a legend nobody read and a tap target split into unclickable quarters. The button's **colour** is now the state — neutral `#9aa0b8` at nothing on, then green warming to orange as the rack fills, with a glow from one effect up — driven by `data-fx-count` and a ramp that lives entirely in `style.css`. A count badge was tried first and dropped: the mixer is scanned for *which tracks are processed*, which hue answers across eight strips at once where a number has to be read one at a time. The roster moved into a hover tooltip (`.fx-tip`) listing every effect and its state, desktop-only (suppressed on `(hover: none), (pointer: coarse)`, where it could never be summoned and a tap would strand it), with a 350 ms dwell so a mouse crossing the Mixer does not strobe eight of them; keyboard focus shows it at once. No `title` — the native tooltip would race the real one and win, slowly — so the state rides in `aria-label` for screen readers. The button no longer owns a row: it shares the volume line (`.voicebank-meta`), and the docked grid lost its `fx` column.

**A Bit crusher placeholder** shows in the popover, greyed with a "Soon" badge and a disabled toggle. Deliberately inert: no params, no persisted flag, no change listener, and nothing in `fx-params.js`, `fx_params.h` or `project-model` — so no half-written field reaches a saved project and there is nothing to migrate when the effect is real. `fxSummary` skips it, so it cannot tint the button.

## 2026-11-12: Collaborate — milestones 3 (relay) and 4 (comforts)

**Other machines (M3-1, M3-2).** `server.py` grew a stub of the room relay the Go service will host: a standard-library WebSocket at `/api/room/<CODE>?peer=<id>` that forwards every text message, unread, to the room's other connections (never echoing), answers pings, caps messages at 32 MB and tells the others `{"relay":"left","peer"}` when a connection drops; the server is now threaded. `relayChannel` is collab.js's default transport: it queues until the socket opens, falls back to the BroadcastChannel when there is no relay at all (so tabs of one browser keep working in production, where the Go service has no `/api/room` yet — **M3-3, parked**), and reconnects a dropped socket with backoff (0.5 s doubling to 5 s). **Heartbeats** every 5 s from everyone; a host named by the relay's `left`, or silent for 20 s, puts the guest in "Waiting for the host…" for a 15 s grace, then "The host has gone" (the project stays on screen); a host back in time → "The host is back". A reconnected guest resyncs with its pending edits rebased; a reconnected host re-snapshots everyone; the host counts a dropped or silent guest out and back in (with a snapshot) on its next beat. The chip shows "Reconnecting…". Transport stays local throughout.

**Comforts (M4).** *Presence in the editors* (M4-1): the clip, tracker head and Mixer strip someone else is editing carry their colour (an outline) and alias (a tag); `presence.js` is the registry collab.js feeds, empty outside a session; `begin`/`presence` carry the colour. *Remote cursors and selections* (M4-2): the tracker cell, the roll's selected notes and the Section pane's picked clips travel at most ten times a second, latest wins, never refereed, and are drawn as coloured ghosts **only inside what is already on screen here** — same section, same pattern; **the local view never follows** a peer (user's rule; a "follow" mode would be a separate opt-in). `cursors.js` is the registry. *Soft locks and takes* (M4-3): recording is **one edit** — `session.beginEdit({ recording: true }, 'record')` at the first recorded tick holds everything the recorder writes until `endEdit('record')` at the stop, then it all travels at once and the referee judges it (its long duration is bounded by the 2-minute cap). Meanwhile everyone else is **warned on every write**, each time, never refused — "⚠ Bob is recording — your change may conflict when the take lands" — and the chip says "● Bob recording"; a write under something another peer has open warns "Bob is editing this too — one of you may lose it". The warning's scope while recording is everything (user: scoping it opens fractal cases).

**Fixed on the way:** a guest's section edits (add, rename, tempo, remove) never travelled — the guest skipped every `section` session event because a plain switch of section is local. Only save notices and collab's own echoes are skipped now.

## 2026-11-12: Collaborate — milestone 2: guests edit, conflicts refereed per atom

**Guests edit the shared project**, and so does the host, with a referee between them. A guest's change shows at once and reaches the host after a quiet moment as an **edit**: groups of compare-and-set ops on **atoms** — a note (`pattern/<id>/note/<start>:<midi>` → duration; no note ids, the lane is never an atom), a pattern field, a track field or effect parameter, a clip field, a section field, the order of sections, a patch, a canvas item. Compatible changes to one pattern merge; a note moved, an entity created or deleted with everything under it, a new pattern with the clip that places it (and a deleted pattern with the clips that played it) stand or fall **as a group**. The host's **referee** judges each op against the body: matching `before` → applied; matching `after` → agreement; otherwise a conflict **only if** the atom's last writer landed after the edit's base version. Conflicts are decided by **earliest start** (then end, then peer id) with **a value beating a deletion** regardless of time, and a start older than two minutes counting as two minutes before its end. Deleted entities keep a **tomb**, so an edit that beats a delete brings the pattern back with its clips. Surviving groups are tried on a scratch copy and the **invariants** checked (slot clash, orphan or overlapping clips, a note outside its pattern); an implicated group is rejected with the reason. Acceptance is per group.

**No clock synchronisation, transport local** (user's call): a guest sends only how long its edit was open; the host stamps start and end on arrival. Play, stop and record never travel; a recording session will be one long edit sent when consolidated, and hearing a collaborator will be streamed audio (TODO › Listen in). "Follow the host's transport" was dropped from M4.

**Only committed state is atomised**: `session.beginEdit(scope, source)` opens an edit (tracker's first keystroke into a pattern, roll's press, section clip grab, mixer fader grab); nothing under its scope is sent until that editor announces the commit, so a tracker pattern travels once its doubles are merged, never mid-typing. `'begin'` events reach only subscribers that ask (`subscribe(fn, { begin: true })`). Guests merge host parts **per atom**, keeping what they have changed and not yet had acked; a rejected group snaps back to the host's value. The host's own edits go through the same referee, so a guest who started first wins against the host.

**People see**: the chip counts conflicts (`⇄ With Jam · ⚠ 1`), the Log gets a line, and the Collaborate dialog has a **Conflicts** table — What (in the project's names: "Riff › C-4 at 2.5", "Keys › volume") · You · Them (who, value, editor, how long) · Kept (who, why) · **Reapply mine** (your value again as a fresh edit on the current base) · Clear. Who is editing what ("Bob is editing Riff") shows on the host and every guest. A **name and colour** are asked before the first Share or Join and stored in the profile.

## 2026-11-02: The Project — a piece of music, exportable, in six phases

A major version change with **no retro-compatibility**: the pre-Project localStorage world is not read, and existing data is wiped. Planned with the user over several turns; the model is theirs.

**The model.** A **Profile** (localStorage, never exported) holds the project index, the current pointer and the global tag vocabulary. A **Project** is one piece of music, its body in IndexedDB, with two dimensions: **Tracks** on the vertical axis, project-level so every section is played by the same band, and an ordered sequence of **Sections** on the horizontal, each arranging **Patterns** as clips on those tracks. "Section" was chosen over "movement" (ambiguous) and "part" (in most DAWs a per-track event container — i.e. this model's *clip* — so it would point at the wrong level). Tempo is the project's with a **nullable per-section override**, the seam the later rit./accel. automation grows from.

**A track is a Mixer strip is a piano-roll voice is a Section lane** — one record, not four kept in step. This killed a live bug: `song.tracks[].mute` gated whether notes were *scheduled* while the voice bank entry's `.muted` gated the channel's *volume*, neither showed the other, and they could disagree. `audibleTracks()` and `effectiveVolume()` now sit side by side so the scheduler's view and the mixer's cannot drift again. Track volume moved to the engine's own 0–127 scale rather than converting at a boundary — converting at a boundary is how the two flags drifted in the first place.

**Persistence.** The open body is the source of truth in memory, so all ~30 synchronous mutators stay synchronous; a debounced flush writes it back whole through a pluggable backend (IndexedDB in the browser, memory in tests). Autosave is every 30 s and only when dirty, skipped while the transport is busy (the clone costs 1.6–4.3 ms of main-thread time — measured — which would jitter note scheduling, not drop audio), and forced when a take ends.

**No "save your changes?" on exit, and there cannot be.** A page cannot show its own `confirm()` during `beforeunload`, and — measured, not assumed — an IndexedDB transaction opened during unload **does not commit**: both handlers run, the transaction is opened inside them, and the page is torn down first. A reload five seconds after typing a note lost the note. localStorage is the only store that survives there, so the last gasp writes the body to a **crash journal** there and the next boot prefers it when it is newer. If even that exceeds the quota the stale journal is dropped (restoring an older copy would look like success) and a marker is left for the next boot to report.

## 2026-11-01: Live tempo changes, and the MIDI panel's two clock settings become one choice

Both halves of the MIDI clock were browser-checked against Reaper. Two bugs came out of it, and the second turned out to be the first.

- **Fixed: a BPM change mid-playback did not move the clock.** The tick worker was told its rate once, in the `start` message, and had no way to change it. Typing a new BPM while the roll ran updated the session — so note *durations* followed it (they read the song on every tick) — but the columns kept arriving at the old rate. **This was also why Reaper would not follow our clock out:** `clockColumn()` re-anchors when `msPerClock` changes, so after a BPM bump it emitted its six ticks at the new tighter spacing while the columns still arrived at the old one. The device saw six fast clocks, a long gap, six fast clocks — unfollowable, and Reaper ignored it. Clock *in* was unaffected and had always worked, because there the tempo is measured from the incoming stream.
- **The worker now retempos a running loop, re-anchoring rather than restarting:** the grid is recomputed from the last tick already emitted, so emitted ticks keep their timing, only pending ones move, and `rawCol` never restarts. On the Atomics path this cannot be a message — the loop blocks in `Atomics.wait` and never returns to its event loop, so a `postMessage` would sit unread until the transport stopped. It travels in the previously-reserved `Int32Array[1]` slot (milli-BPM) with the existing `notify` cutting the current sleep short; the `setTimeout` fallback takes a `{type:'tempo'}` message. In both paths the target is recomputed *after* waking and *before* the precision spin — otherwise a tempo drop gets spun through at the old, shorter interval and the tick fires early. `setTickWorkerTempo()` in app.js, threaded through as the transport's `tempoWorker`; the transport pushes it from `refresh()`, which is already the "the song changed under us" entry point both BPM inputs reach.
- **Fixed: "Send MIDI clock" could sit ticked and send nothing.** `isClockOutEnabled()` is `sendClock && !syncToClock && port`, but the two were independent checkboxes — so leaving *Sync to external clock* on from driving us *from* a DAW made clock-out silently inert, with nothing on screen saying why. They are now **one choice**, enforced in midi-manager rather than the UI: switching either on switches the other off (and becoming a slave stops whatever device was being driven). `initFromStorage()` repairs the both-on state an older build may have persisted — sync wins. The `!syncToClock` guard stays as an invariant, now unreachable through the setters.
- **New: clock out warns when there is no output device.** The one remaining way to tick the box and get nothing.
- **The MIDI panel is restructured by signal direction:** Device type first, then an **Input** section (Input, Input channel, Sync to external clock) and an **Output** section (Output, Device channel, Play notes through, slot map, Send MIDI clock, Output latency, Audition, Panic). Clock now sits with its direction, which is also what makes the two boxes read as the halves of one choice. New `.midi-section` / `.midi-section-title` styles. The send-clock hint now says outright that it is independent of "Play notes through" — the clock goes out with the notes staying internal.
- Tests: `test/midi-clock-in.test.js`'s "a slave does not send clock" asserted the exact trap ("the setting is kept… but it is not acted on") and was replaced by both directions of the new exclusion plus the legacy-storage repair (**734 unit passing**). `e2e/transport.spec.js` gains a tempo-change case measuring the column rate either side of a mid-playback doubling — confirmed to fail on the old behaviour; `e2e/midi.spec.js` gains the exclusion through the real checkboxes, the no-output warning, and clock streaming with **Internal synth only** selected while zero note-ons reach the port (**54 e2e passing**).

## 2026-10-31: VoiceBank de-flaking — one write path, slot badges, the Mixer tab

The relation between engine patches, the VoiceBank, the playing pattern and the Song editor's tracks was flaky. Root cause was not one bug but an architecture: **four uncoordinated writers** into a bank whose array was `splice`d even though the slot index *is* the synth channel.

- **Fixed: removing a voice silently repointed every track below it.** `removeEntry` (and the drop-dedup path) spliced the entries array, so each entry below shifted channel *and* was recoloured by `reindexColors` — a Song track bound to `slot: 3` started playing slot 4's voice, and the roll's note colours moved under the user. The bank is now a fixed `Array(8)`; removal leaves a `null` hole. Holes survive reload and song save/restore (`serializeBank` keeps them, trimming only the tail).
- **Fixed: duplicates could enter the bank.** Both dedup paths compared `string===string` or `Array===Array` and returned false for any mismatched pair, so a `Uint8Array` entry never matched an `Array` one; `addEntryAt`/`insertEntry`/`_setEntries` skipped dedup entirely.
- **New `voiceKey(voiceData)`** in patch-model.js: a name-independent hash of the patch bytes (name chars zeroed — 118–127 packed, 145–154 VCED), normalised across base64 / `Uint8Array` / `Array`. Unlike patch ids, which deliberately include the name so two records can coexist, **one voiceKey means one slot**. Carries its own base64 decoder — `atob` is not in the node harness.
- **One write path.** `assignToSlot(idx, patch)` is the only mutator. Semantics decided with the user: target occupied + voice unassigned → **silent overwrite**; voice already in another slot → **swap** (both entries move wholesale, keeping ids, names and volumes). Removed: drag-a-patch-into-the-bank, the internal reorder handle, `addEntryAt`/`insertEntry`, the auto-add on *playing* a patch, and the full-row-click assign. Auditioning a patch no longer touches the bank.
- **The ♫ assign popover** (`attachAssignPopover`, also on canvas container headers): eight buttons showing each slot's occupant and colour, `.current` on the patch's own slot, first empty pre-focused; a click commits and closes, Escape or an outside click dismisses.
- **Slot badges on every instance of a patch.** A `voiceKey → {slot, colour}` registry, rebuilt from each bank snapshot, drives a pill on every patch row — canvas, container, header. Identity is the voice bytes, so a renamed copy and the same voice in three containers all wear the same number and move together. Refresh is **by document query, not subscription**: rows are rebuilt on every canvas render, so a subscriber list would accumulate dead elements.
- **New Mixer tab** (🎚️, desktop *and* mobile — the old mobile-only `compact` mode is gone). The bank left the pattern-editor row and renders eight vertical channel strips: slot pill, name, vertical fader, volume, and M / S / ▶ / ×.

## 2026-10-30: MIDI clock in — following an external device

- **"Sync to external clock"** in the MIDI settings (off by default). With it on, Play and Record only **arm**: the Play button pulses amber, nothing runs, and the device's Start runs us — from the top — at the device's tempo, measured from its clock (the mean of the last quarter note's worth of periods). Its Stop pauses us and leaves us armed, so its next Start or Continue is followed too; Continue resumes from where we were. Our own Stop disarms. **Song Position is ignored**, by decision.
- Realtime bytes (0xF8 and up) are routed to the clock before SysEx and notes — they may legally arrive mid-SysEx, and the note handler drops them anyway. A slave **does not send clock** (it would loop straight back onto the bus), and switching to slave while clocking a device stops that device first.
- The recorder's time base is now the moment the transport actually begins a take, not the moment Rec was pressed, and its column arithmetic uses the transport's effective tempo: under an external clock the two moments can be seconds apart and the tempos differ. (Rec on a stopped transport is a step record; Play then Rec arms a take.) A take into an empty song still starts on the device's Start.
- `test/midi-clock-in.test.js`; verified in a browser with a hand-driven fake input: armed → Start → columns → device Stop (paused, armed) → Continue → our Stop → a later Start ignored. This closes the "basic internal clock sending / syncing with external" item.

## 2026-10-30: MIDI clock out

- **"Send MIDI clock"** in the MIDI settings (off by default): the app's transport drives an external sequencer or drum machine. Start, Stop, and 24 ticks to the quarter note at the song's tempo, from both Play and Record. Starting anywhere but the top sends a Song Position first and Continues from it — a MIDI Song Position counts sixteenths, which is exactly our columns, so a loop starting at bar 2 tells the device so.
- The tick worker wakes once per column (a sixteenth), so each column is scheduled as **six timestamped clocks in one batch** rather than run off a 24-PPQN timer of our own. Their times come from a base anchored at the first column, not from each tick's own arrival: a device reads the gaps as the tempo, so worker jitter would be audible wobble, and a late batch could otherwise be stamped behind the batch before it. The base re-anchors on a tempo change. Measured in a browser against a fake port: 48 clocks in a second at 120 BPM, every gap 20.833 ms.
- Clock goes out through the same path as notes, so it carries the output latency and the device stays in step with what you hear. It is deliberately independent of "Play notes through" — clocking a drum machine while listening to the internal synth is an ordinary thing to want. Panic stops it, which is what a closing tab calls.
- `test/midi-clock.test.js`. The audition checkbox gained a `.midi-audition` class so a panel test stops identifying it as "the first checkbox".

## 2026-10-29: Patterns, Tracks and the Song editor

The sequencer is rebuilt around reusable **patterns**. Six phases, one commit each.

- **Model** (`html/components/song-model.js`) — Pattern (a bar or more of notes, monotimbral: notes carry no voice), Track (bound to a voice-bank slot = synth channel = MIDI channel), Clip (a pattern on a track at a bar, with repeats), Song (tracks + clips + bpm + loop). Factories are total, so stored or hand-edited data comes back well-formed. Id-keyed stores, so names are free and two patterns may share one. One clip per track per bar.
- **Migration** — the old flat roll (whose per-note `slot` was the voice) becomes one pattern + track per used slot; legacy saved songs convert the same way, keeping their voice bank. Versioned and idempotent; the legacy keys are never deleted.
- **Sequencer core** (`html/synth/sequencer.js`) — pure: given a song and its patterns, what starts at column N and on which slot. Patterns are snapshotted at compile time (reading one parses localStorage, which must never happen on a tick); mute/solo are read live. Solo beats mute.
- **Pattern editor** — the piano roll now edits one pattern, the active voice's. Other tracks draw as read-only ghosts behind it. `notes` became a binding repointed at the active pattern, which is why the editing layer survived the change untouched.
- **Song editor** (`html/components/song-editor.js`, Song tab) — tracks × bars grid, pattern palette, place/move/repeat/remove clips, per-track voice + mute/solo, song select/name/BPM/loop. Autosaves; model refusals ("Clip overlaps") surface in a status line. A shared `song-session.js` owns the current song so neither editor overwrites the other.
- **Transport** (`html/synth/transport.js`) — one owner of the clock, subscribed to the tick worker exactly once (previously the roll and the toolbar each drove it). Modes: Song (default, and identical to the old behaviour) and Pattern. Drives both playheads and recording; recording still sounds the whole arrangement as a backing.
- **Cleanup** — `song-bar.js` and the legacy `songStore` removed; the voice bank now follows the song on a switch; tutorial and HEARME.tts updated.

Caught in review along the way: recording into an empty song dropped the note; the tutorial was opening the wrong tab after the Song tab shifted the indices; and songs saved through the old song bar after the first migration would have been stranded by its removal.
